# Writing standard: IEA World Energy Outlook

The reference model for prose quality is the IEA World Energy Outlook. The audience is government decision-makers (ministers, boards, institutional investors).

**English only.** All chapter prose, headings, captions, tables of contents, callouts, and figure notes are written in **English**, using International English unless a sponsor specifies US or UK spelling. Do not draft in another language and translate later; do not leave Romanian (or other) filler, hedges, or stock phrases in the manuscript. Every rule below applies to all chapter text produced in this project.

**Voice and tone.** Write as a senior energy policy analyst briefing a smart but busy energy minister. Authoritative, not arrogant. Direct, not simplistic. Every sentence must advance an argument or deliver verifiable information. No filler. Active voice by default.

**Sentence and paragraph craft.** Vary sentence length: short declarative sentences (8-12 words) for conclusions, longer ones (20-35 words) for context. Never exceed ~40 words. Each paragraph: one main idea, 3-6 sentences. First sentence introduces the idea directly (not a rhetorical question or empty framing). Last sentence draws a conclusion or bridges to the next idea.

**Data in prose.** Integrate numbers naturally: provide scale and comparison, not just the figure. Use tables for structured datasets; prose interprets and contextualises.

**Uncertainty and confidence.** Express uncertainty through ranges, scenarios, confidence levels, review requirements, or explicit conditions. Write diplomatically and preserve confidence in the report. State what further review would improve, which aspect it would test, and why it is a normal Stage 3 or human-review task.

**Language and loanwords.** Keep the manuscript in English end-to-end. Retain established international technical terms in English (capacity factor, CfD, LCOE, baseload, merit order, load-following, dunkelflaute, firm power, strike price, and project-specific rubric codes such as NH-01). When citing Romanian or other non-English regulations, quote the original briefly and give the English gloss at first use.

**AI-marks blacklist (English prose).** BANNED: the Unicode em dash character U+2014 anywhere in reader-facing text; "It is important to note that..."; "It is worth highlighting that..."; "Last but not least..."; "A noteworthy aspect is that..."; "plays a crucial / essential / vital role"; "in a rapidly evolving landscape"; "increasing complexity"; "significant challenges"; "transformative potential"; "undoubtedly"; definitions by negation such as "the objective is not X, it is Y". Also banned: more than two consecutive sentences starting with the same syntactic pattern, bullets where flowing prose would be more natural, parenthetical asides that duplicate information already in the sentence, and overuse of intensifiers such as significant, substantial, and remarkable. Do not substitute Romanian or other-language equivalents of these patterns; they are equally out of scope for an English report.

**Positive, direct phrasing.** State the affirmative claim first. Use concise institutional prose. Avoid setting up an argument by saying what the report does not do, except where a legal or scope boundary requires it.

**Citations.** Every factual or numerical claim links to a public source or an explicit hypothesis with ID. Use Harvard-style citations in text, for example `(IAEA, 2015)` or `(U.S. DOE, 2022, p. 14)`, with a consolidated Harvard-style reference list. Do not cite repository paths, markdown files, internal project files, run IDs, working notes, or the GEM database in the reader-facing report.

**Quality test before finalising.** (1) Can a decision-maker extract a conclusion or useful fact from each paragraph? If not, rewrite. (2) Remove any sentence that adds no new information. (3) Read the first 3 sentences of each section aloud; if they sound generic, rewrite with more specificity. (4) Verify every number has a source.
