# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Purpose of the Report

The report supports public decision-makers in identifying coal and other thermal power plant sites that merit detailed evaluation for small modular reactor deployment. It applies the IAEA site survey and site selection sequence to a broad brownfield site universe, then narrows that universe through exclusionary screening, comparative scoring, national sensitivity analysis, and country-level interpretation (IAEA, 2015; EPRI, 2022).

The immediate decision is a Stage 1 and Stage 2 decision: which sites deserve progression toward detailed site characterisation. The report therefore provides a disciplined screening and ranking basis for governments, energy ministries, regulators, utilities, and public agencies considering coal-to-nuclear transition options. It does not establish site suitability, licence readiness, procurement suitability, financing viability, or construction approval.

NuScale VOYGR-6 is the only reference deployment envelope used in the analysis. The reference case gives the screening method a consistent capacity, footprint, grid, cooling, transport, and emergency-planning basis. It does not imply vendor selection, procurement intent, or host-country licensing acceptance.

## 1.2 Scope and Boundaries: IAEA Stages 1 and 2 Only

IAEA SSG-35 describes a staged process that begins with regional analysis and site survey, moves through site selection, and then proceeds to progressively more detailed characterisation and evaluation (IAEA, 2015). This report covers the first two stages. Stage 3 and later activities remain the responsibility of national authorities, project sponsors, regulators, operators, and technical specialists working with site-specific evidence.

| Stage | Name | Treatment in this report |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Site Survey | Defines the study region, identifies potential sites, and screens the site universe into a candidate population. |
| 2 | Site Selection | Evaluates, compares, ranks, and interprets candidate sites to support selection for detailed follow-up. |
| 3 | Site Characterisation | Identified as the next work programme for selected sites; detailed field investigations and design-basis studies sit here. |
| 4 | Pre-operational | Outside the report scope; requires confirmatory measurements and monitoring before operation. |
| 5 | Operational | Outside the report scope; concerns long-term monitoring and periodic safety review. |

The Stage 1 and Stage 2 outputs are sufficient for prioritisation. They support decisions about where to spend further public and technical effort, which issues require early regulator engagement, and which candidate sites should be moved into a site-specific work programme. They do not establish design-basis ground motion, design-basis flood levels, licensing-quality geotechnical parameters, approved emergency plans, final environmental acceptability, or public consent.

## 1.3 Coal-to-Nuclear Transition Context

Coal-to-nuclear transition is relevant because coal and thermal power plant sites can concentrate assets that are costly and slow to recreate on greenfield land. Existing industrial zoning, transmission corridors, cooling-water context, transport access, workforce relationships, and host-community familiarity with power generation can make some brownfield sites attractive starting points for Stage 1 and Stage 2 screening.

The U.S. Department of Energy frames coal-to-nuclear transition as a pathway for retaining energy-community economic value while replacing retiring coal generation with firm low-carbon electricity (U.S. DOE, 2024). DOE-supported analysis also identifies potential reuse value in transmission, cooling, roads, rail, land, and workforce relationships, while emphasising that each site still requires technical, environmental, regulatory, and community review (INL, 2022).

For government readers, the transition question is practical. A candidate site is stronger when existing assets reduce later project uncertainty and weaker when brownfield constraints introduce safety, environmental, emergency-planning, ownership, or stakeholder risks. The report tests that balance at screening resolution and identifies where detailed Stage 3 characterisation would add the most value.

## 1.4 Regulatory and Methodological Framework: IAEA, EPRI, and Project Alignment

IAEA safety standards provide the primary nuclear siting framework. SSG-35 defines the site survey and site selection process used in the report, while SSR-1 establishes the broader site-evaluation discipline that later stages must satisfy (IAEA, 2015; IAEA, 2019). Supporting IAEA safety guides inform treatment of seismic, geotechnical, hydrological, meteorological, volcanic, human-induced, radiological, and emergency-planning considerations.

EPRI's advanced nuclear siting guide provides the practical bridge from safety standards to a structured screening workflow for advanced nuclear projects (EPRI, 2022). It distinguishes exclusionary screening, avoidance screening, suitability evaluation, and alternative-site ranking. The report uses the same logic: hard exclusionary issues and safety floors are considered before weighted scoring, and weighted scoring is used only as a comparative tool for site/technology pairs that remain inside the Stage 1 and Stage 2 candidate set.

| Theme | IAEA framing | EPRI / industry framing | Project stance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Process stage | Site survey and site selection lead into progressive site evaluation. | Pre-screening, exclusionary screening, avoidance screening, suitability evaluation, and ranking. | Treat the report as a Stage 1 and Stage 2 decision-support product. |
| Criteria families | Safety-related, nuclear security, and non-safety-related criteria. | Practical siting categories for advanced nuclear deployment. | Organise criteria by safety family, then retain implementation criteria where they affect brownfield feasibility. |
| Exclusion and ranking | Exclusionary, discretionary, and ranking criteria. | Exclusionary screening, avoidance screening, suitability evaluation, and alternative-site ranking. | Apply exclusionary gates and safety floors before composite ranking. |
| Level of evidence | Evidence detail increases as the project moves toward characterisation. | Screening and ranking precede detailed engineering. | Use screening-grade and ranking-grade evidence for prioritisation, then route unresolved questions to Stage 3. |
| Robustness | Good siting practice requires explicit treatment of uncertainty and assumptions. | Rankings should be tested against sensitivity to weights and assumptions. | Use the project's national sensitivity analysis to interpret rank robustness and sequencing. |

The report also separates nuclear-safety criteria from project-implementation criteria. Grid connection, heavy-haul transport, land area, workforce, ownership context, infrastructure reuse, and coal-to-nuclear synergy matter to programme feasibility. They do not replace SSR-1 safety expectations and they do not override exclusionary or safety-floor findings.

## 1.5 Data, Scoring, and National Sensitivity Overview

The evidence base combines site inventory records, public geospatial and infrastructure evidence, hazard indicators, population and emergency-planning proxies, country context, documented assumptions, scoring outputs, and human review. The report describes those sources qualitatively because the public deliverable is a decision-support report rather than a catalogue of source systems.

At report scale, the method has four layers. The first layer is the candidate inventory: coal and thermal power plant records with location, operating status, capacity, ownership, grid, and country attributes where available. The second layer is evidence enrichment: screening-grade and ranking-grade indicators for hazards, radiological impact, emergency planning, infrastructure, land, environment, ownership, and reuse context. The third layer is quality control: source hierarchy, evidence grading, uncertainty labels, and assumption tracking. The fourth layer is evaluation: exclusionary checks, safety floors, weighted scoring, national rank interpretation, and sensitivity testing.

The scoring method uses a dual gate. A site/technology pair that fails an exclusionary condition or safety floor cannot be made acceptable by strong non-safety attributes. Surviving candidates are then compared through criterion scores and a composite ranking. This preserves the distinction between safety screening and programme attractiveness.

The project's 50,000-iteration national Monte Carlo sensitivity analysis is the controlling robustness frame for country and site choices. It tests how rankings move under uncertainty in scores and weights within each country's candidate set. Country profiles, selected-site profiles, and Stage 3 sequencing therefore use national rank stability, national score bands, and national top-tier persistence within the host-country candidate pool.

NuScale VOYGR-6 remains the single reference deployment envelope. The reference case is 462 MWe and is used consistently in screening, ranking, land, grid, cooling, transport, and Stage 3 sequencing language.

| Criterion family | Screening role | Main Stage 1-2 limit | Treatment in this report |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Natural hazards | Identify seismic, geotechnical, hydrological, meteorological, volcanic, and combined-hazard concerns. | Desk-scale evidence does not replace site-specific hazard characterisation. | Use for screening and ranking; route design-basis values to Stage 3. |
| Human-induced and security-relevant hazards | Screen aircraft, industrial, transport, military, electromagnetic, and external event concerns. | Public evidence may miss restricted or locally classified activity. | Treat as screening evidence; require national authority confirmation where material. |
| Radiological impact and emergency planning | Assess dispersion proxies, population context, evacuation feasibility, and special populations. | Screening does not constitute a dose assessment or emergency plan. | Use for comparison; require regulator-facing modelling and emergency-planning work at Stage 3. |
| Non-safety and implementation criteria | Capture grid, cooling, transport, land, ownership, environment, workforce, and reuse conditions. | Several factors indicate programme feasibility rather than nuclear safety. | Use for prioritisation and policy interpretation after safety gates are respected. |
| Data quality and assumptions | Explain evidence strength, missing values, and sensitivity to uncertainty. | Some indicators remain screening-grade or unscored. | Preserve uncertainty in score bands, confidence labels, and Stage 3 follow-up actions. |

## 1.6 Structure of the Report

The report follows the IAEA Stage 1 and Stage 2 sequence. Chapter 2 presents the site survey stage: study region, site universe, evidence base, eligibility checks, candidate identification, and Stage 1 outputs. Chapter 3 presents the site selection stage: criterion families, screening and scoring logic, national sensitivity treatment, and the shortlist rationale for the NuScale VOYGR-6 reference case.

Chapter 4 presents the cross-country results and main drivers of suitability, exclusion, uncertainty, and national stability. Chapter 5 provides the accepted country packets and selected-site profiles for the published country set. Chapter 6 converts the screening findings into a Stage 3 work programme. Chapter 7 closes with final remarks on how the screening evidence should be used. Chapter 8 provides the consolidated external reference list.

The annexes carry the methodological backbone: IAEA and EPRI traceability, exclusionary floors, scoring methods, national sensitivity methods, failure-mode analysis, assumption register, and generated artefact index. A separate executive technical brief may be prepared for governance and audit audiences, but the main report remains the client-facing decision document.
