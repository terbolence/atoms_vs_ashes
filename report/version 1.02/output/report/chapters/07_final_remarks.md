# 7. Final Remarks

The report gives governments a structured way to move from a broad brownfield site universe to a defensible set of sites for detailed evaluation. It combines IAEA Stage 1 site survey logic, Stage 2 site selection logic, exclusionary and avoidance screening, comparative scoring, and the project's 50,000-iteration national sensitivity analysis. The result is a prioritisation basis for Stage 3 characterisation, not a licence finding or construction decision.

The main value lies in traceability. A reader can see why a site remains in consideration, which criteria support its position, which criteria hold it back, how stable its national rank is under sensitivity testing, and which evidence gaps require field confirmation. That chain matters more than small differences between adjacent composite scores. It turns the shortlist into a managed work programme rather than a static ranking.

The scope remains Stage 1 and Stage 2. Site-specific ground investigation, design-basis hazard studies, emergency plan approval, grid connection offers, environmental permitting, land control, public engagement, and licensing decisions belong to Stage 3 and later national processes. A recommendation to progress a site means that the screening evidence justifies spending effort on those processes. It does not mean that the site is suitable for construction.

Uncertainty is an input to the decision. Screening evidence is expected to contain proxies, unscored criteria, quality labels, and values that need national confirmation. The report preserves that uncertainty through evidence coverage, score bands, national stability bands, residual-risk registers, and Stage 3 follow-up actions. The right response is targeted investigation, not over-reading the point estimate.

Coal and thermal sites are a rational starting point because many already carry grid, cooling, transport, land, industrial, and workforce attributes that can improve the economics and schedule of later evaluation. Those attributes are useful only when they sit alongside acceptable safety, security, radiological, emergency-planning, environmental, and stakeholder conditions. The framework therefore keeps brownfield reuse advantages inside the same disciplined screening structure as every safety-related concern.

The same framework can be expanded to other large industrial sites that may be suitable for SMR accommodation. Such expansion should preserve the same gates: clear site universe, documented evidence, exclusionary screening, national sensitivity analysis, residual-risk interpretation, and Stage 3 confirmation. The method is portable because it is auditable, but its conclusions remain site-specific.

The final decision belongs to national institutions. Governments, regulators, operators, local authorities, land owners, and the public will determine whether any candidate proceeds from screening to characterisation and from characterisation toward a licence-quality case. This report provides the starting set and the next-step sequence for that decision.
