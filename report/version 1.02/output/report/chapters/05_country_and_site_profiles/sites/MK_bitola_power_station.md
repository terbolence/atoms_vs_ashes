# Bitola power station Site Profile

Bitola power station is a coal/thermal site in North Macedonia that has been tested against the NuScale VOYGR-6 reference deployment envelope. This profile reads the screening evidence at a level that supports a decision to progress toward Stage 3 characterisation. It is not a site-suitability determination, vendor recommendation, or licensing finding.

### Interpretation - Natural Hazards (NH)


Natural hazards at Bitola are favourable in several desk-screening dimensions, but Seismic: Ground Motion (NH-01) is the binding natural-hazard question. The 475-year PGA is 0.255 g and the 2,475-year PGA is 0.544 g, which places the site above the project's avoidance trigger for the long-return-period ground-motion screen. Surface rupture is less severe at this stage: the nearest mapped capable fault is 11.4 km away, and the screening file records a 0.173 mm/yr slip rate for MKCF003. The supporting geotechnical file is mixed but workable for Stage 1-2. Liquefaction susceptibility is moderate, bearing capacity is 86.7 kPa, depth to bedrock is 26.1 m, and the site slope is a gentle 2.38 degrees. Wider natural-hazard conditions are stronger: the flood-zone class is negligible, the coast is 115.1 km away, the 50-year screening wind-speed index is 7.43 m/s, and the volcanic hazard class is negligible. The precipitation values, 20.3 mm/yr screening precipitation index and 0.17 mm screening extreme-precipitation index, are useful only as screening evidence and should be re-checked before design use. Stage 3 should start with site-specific PSHA using measured Vs30, then confirm local flood, precipitation and drainage inputs and run a CPT/SPT geotechnical campaign for liquefaction and foundation design.

### Interpretation - Human-Induced and Security-Relevant Hazards (HI)


Human-induced and security-relevant hazards at Bitola are led by aviation and RF/EMI screening issues, not by the broader industrial-hazard file. Aircraft Crash (HI-01) scores 3.5/10 because Logovardi Sport Aerodrome is 6.64 km from the site and the nearest flight-path proxy is 3.32 km away; both values require a project-specific aircraft-crash hazard assessment before the avoidance flag can be retired. Industrial Explosions (HI-02), Toxic/Gas Releases (HI-03), and External Fires (HI-04) each score 9.5/10 in the frozen scoring run, indicating no standing adverse signal in the available screening evidence. Military Installations (HI-06) is a moderate concern rather than the lead issue: the nearest military feature is 16.2 km away, with 10 features inside the wider screening radius. Electromagnetic Interference (HI-07) is more material, with a transmitter 0.22 km from the site and 26 transmitters in the search radius. Stage 3 should therefore commission the aircraft-crash assessment for Logovardi, complete an RF/EMI field survey, and refresh the hazardous-neighbour and defence-feature inventories with the responsible national authorities.

### Interpretation - Radiological Impact and Emergency Planning (RI / EP)


Radiological impact and emergency-planning conditions at Bitola are helped by low population density but constrained by the EPZ road network and unmeasured liquid-pathway evidence. Emergency Planning Feasibility (EP-01) is marked feasible with a 67.3/100 composite, but the road sub-score is only 38.1/100. Population density is favourable for screening, at 22.2 people/km<sup>2</sup> within 5 km and 54.9 people/km<sup>2</sup> within 25 km; the 25 km population is 107,711 people and the 60-year projection is 72,283 people. Surface Water Dispersion (RI-02) remains a 3.5/10 question with no measured values in the table. Stage 3 should model EPZ clearance times, install a local meteorological measurement programme, and quantify Crna Reka and canal-system dilution at low-flow conditions.

### Interpretation - Non-Safety and Implementation Considerations (NS)


Non-safety and implementation conditions are Bitola's main strength, with a strong grid and land signal offset by land-cover, transport and ecological-evidence gaps. The grid file is the clearest advantage: the nearest substation is 0.99 km away, the nearest high-voltage line is 0.54 km away, the highest nearby line voltage is 400 kV, and inherited export capacity is 699 MW. Cooling Water Availability (NS-01) is also usable at Stage 1-2, with the X Канал source 3.99 km from the site, 22.9 m<sup>3</sup>/s flow, and a Low-Medium water-stress label. The site snapshot records 145.7 ha of available surface area and 145.7 ha currently available for development, so the land-footprint screen is favourable. The caution is that Site Topography (NS-04) records 0% favourable, moderate and unfavourable land-cover values, which reads as a measurement gap rather than a physical absence of land. Ecological Sensitivity (NS-08) is a Stage 3 evidence item: no protected-area overlap is recorded, the nearest protected area is 8.618 km away, and data quality is insufficient. Stage 3 should reconcile land-cover mapping with the 145.7 ha development estimate, confirm cooling-water reliability under dry-year conditions, and complete transport, ecological and ownership/legal due diligence.

## Composite Score and Stability

Baseline composite score is 6.812, bracketed by Monte Carlo at 5.927-7.329. National stability band is `A` with a top-10% hit rate of 100% across 12 scored Monte Carlo scenarios.

Bitola's baseline composite score is 6.812, with a Monte Carlo bracket of 5.927-7.329. The bracket is moderately wide, but the national sensitivity signal is strong: Bitola is the only Macedonian site in stability band A and records a 100% national top-10 hit rate across the 12 scored national sensitivity scenarios. In programme terms, that means Bitola remains the national leader under the weight perturbations considered, rather than depending on one narrow baseline-weight choice. The composite is supported by grid connection, land area, population-density and natural-hazard strengths, while the main drag comes from EP-02, HI-01, HI-07, NH-01 and the NS-04 land-cover evidence gap. Stage 3 effort should therefore concentrate on the few measurements most likely to narrow the composite band: PSHA, aircraft-crash analysis, EPZ road modelling, EMI survey work, and land-cover verification.
