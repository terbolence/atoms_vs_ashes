# Document Map: IAEA SSG-21
**Title:** Volcanic Hazards in Site Evaluation for Nuclear Installations  
**Series:** IAEA Safety Standards Series No. SSG-21 (Specific Safety Guide)  
**Year:** 2012 | **Pages:** 128

---

## Purpose
Provides recommendations on evaluating volcanic hazards at nuclear installation sites to meet SSR-1 (NS-R-3) Requirement 17. Covers screening, capability assessment, hazard analysis, and design basis for volcanic phenomena.

---

## Section Map

### Section 1: Introduction (pp. 1–5)
- Applies to all nuclear installations via graded approach.
- Addresses both new sites and existing installations.

### Section 2: General Recommendations (volcanic hazard framework)
- Initial screening to determine if site is within a volcanic region.
- If volcanic, comprehensive volcanic hazard assessment required.
- Must consider all volcanic phenomena that could reach the site.

### Section 3: Volcanic Hazard Screening
- Screening distance based on volcano type and historical activity.
- Holocene volcanoes (last 11,700 years) are primary concern.
- Must investigate the complete geological record of each relevant volcano.

### Section 4: Capability Assessment
- Determine if volcano is capable of future activity.
- Evaluate eruption history, repose intervals, magma composition.
- Assess potential eruption scenarios and magnitudes.

### Section 5: Volcanic Hazard Assessment
- **Volcanic phenomena to evaluate:**
  - Tephra fallout (ash fall) — most widespread hazard
  - Pyroclastic flows and surges — high-energy, extremely destructive
  - Lava flows — terrain-following, generally slow-moving
  - Lahars (volcanic mudflows) — can travel tens of km from source
  - Volcanic gases — SO₂, CO₂, HF, HCl
  - Ballistic projectiles — near-vent hazard
  - Ground deformation — uplift, subsidence, faulting
  - Volcanic earthquakes
  - Volcanic tsunamis (flank collapse, caldera collapse)
  - Atmospheric effects (lightning, shock waves)
- Probabilistic and deterministic approaches for each phenomenon.

### Section 6: Design Basis Determination
- Screening distance values for different volcanic phenomena.
- Return period requirements for volcanic hazards.

### Section 7: Monitoring and Emergency
- Volcano monitoring recommendations for nearby installations.

## Key Siting Criteria Extracted
- **Exclusionary hazards (per SSG-35 Table I-1):** Lava flow, pyroclastic flow, massive lahars, ground deformation → site exclusion if within hazard zone.
- **Discretionary/ranking:** Tephra fall, volcanic gases → engineering solutions may exist.
- **Screening radius:** Depends on volcano type; can extend to 100+ km for ash fall.
- **For Central/Eastern/Southern Europe:** Volcanic hazard is generally low but must be verified for each site (e.g., Carpathian volcanic arc, Greek volcanic arc).
