# Document Map: IAEA SSG-18
**Title:** Meteorological and Hydrological Hazards in Site Evaluation for Nuclear Installations  
**Series:** IAEA Safety Standards Series No. SSG-18 (Specific Safety Guide)  
**Year:** 2011 | **Pages:** 172 | **Joint sponsor:** WMO  
**Supersedes:** NS-G-3.4 (2003) and NS-G-3.5 (2003)

---

## Purpose
Provides recommendations on assessing meteorological and hydrological hazards at nuclear installation sites to meet SSR-1 (NS-R-3) Requirements 18, 19, and 20. Covers extreme weather, flooding, tsunami, low water conditions, and climate change.

---

## Section Map

### Section 1: Introduction (pp. 1–5, §1.1–1.14)
- Combines guidance from former NS-G-3.4 (meteorological) and NS-G-3.5 (flooding).
- Applies to all nuclear installations via graded approach.
- Note on Fukushima: pre-dates March 2011 event; future revisions to reflect lessons.

### Section 2: General Considerations and Recommendations (pp. 5–14, §2.1–2.39)
- **Meteorological hazards (§2.6–2.10):** Air temperature, wind speed, precipitation, snowpack extremes. Rare phenomena: lightning, tropical cyclones, tornadoes, waterspouts. Other: dust storms, hail, freezing precipitation.
- **Hydrological hazards (§2.11–2.17):** Storm surge, wind waves, tsunami, seiche, river flooding, dam break, flash flood, ice effects, high groundwater, low water conditions.
- **Combined hazards (§2.18–2.22):** Must consider dependent combinations (e.g., storm surge + wind, drought + high temperature).
- **Climate change (§2.23–2.27):** Must account for projected changes over plant lifetime.
- **Return periods and exceedance probabilities (§2.28–2.39):** Typically 10⁻⁴/year or rarer for design basis.

### Section 3: Databases (pp. 15–28, §3.1–3.40)
- **Meteorological data (§3.11–3.26):** Minimum 30 years of observations. Parameters: temperature, humidity, wind, precipitation, snow, pressure. Sources: national met services, WMO, satellites.
- **Hydrological data (§3.27–3.40):** River discharge, water levels, tidal records, storm surge, wave, tsunami catalogues. Bathymetric and topographic data.

### Section 4: Assessment of Meteorological Hazards (pp. 29–44, §4.1–4.73)
- **Extreme phenomena (§4.4–4.31):** Statistical analysis of extremes for temperature, wind, precipitation, snowpack. Use Gumbel/GEV distributions.
- **Rare phenomena (§4.32–4.62):** Tornado assessment (Fujita scale, probability models), tropical cyclone (parametric models), lightning (flash density maps), waterspouts.
- **Other phenomena (§4.63–4.73):** Dust/sandstorms, hail, freezing rain, frost.

### Section 5: Assessment of Hydrological Hazards (pp. 45–77, §5.1–5.137)
- **Storm surges (§5.1–5.16):** Parametric models, historical analysis, numerical modelling.
- **Wind waves (§5.17–5.35):** Wave prediction, run-up calculations.
- **Tsunamis (§5.36–5.69):** Source characterization, numerical propagation modelling, inundation mapping. **Screening criteria (§5.41): Site ≥10 km from ocean shore, ≥1 km from lake/fjord, or ≥50 m above MSL.**
- **Seiches (§5.70–5.77):** Enclosed/semi-enclosed water body oscillation.
- **Extreme precipitation/river flooding (§5.78–5.103):** PMF estimation, rainfall-runoff models, dam break analysis.
- **Dam break floods (§5.104–5.127):** Sunny-day and seismically triggered dam failure scenarios.
- **Bores and mechanically induced waves (§5.128–5.132).**
- **High groundwater (§5.133–5.137).**

### Section 6: Design Basis Parameters (pp. 78–82, §6.1–6.16)
- Meteorological design basis: extreme values at required return periods.
- Hydrological design basis: design basis flood level (DBFL), including combinations.

### Section 7: Site Protection Measures (pp. 83–89, §7.1–7.25)
- Permanent and temporary flood defences, drainage, shoreline protection.

### Section 8: Changes in Hazards with Time (pp. 90–91, §8.1–8.11)
- Climate evolution, land use changes, upstream/downstream developments.

### Section 9: Monitoring and Warning Systems (pp. 92–95, §9.1–9.21)
- On-site meteorological and hydrological monitoring requirements.

### Section 10: Non-NPP Installations (pp. 96–99, §10.1–10.10)
- Graded approach guidance.

### Section 11: Management System (pp. 100–102, §11.1–11.17)
- Project organization, peer review.

### Annex I: Example Design Basis Parameters (pp. 107–112)
- Example return periods and criteria for meteorological variables by country.

### Annex II: Tsunami Assessment Current Practice (pp. 113–127)
- Country-specific approaches (Japan, India, USA, France).

## Key Siting Criteria Extracted
- **Tsunami screening:** ≥10 km from ocean shore, ≥1 km from lake/fjord, or ≥50 m above MSL
- **Return periods:** Typically 10⁻⁴/year for design basis flood and extreme meteorological events
- **Climate change:** Must be considered over the 60-year NuScale design life
- **Minimum data:** 30 years meteorological observations recommended
