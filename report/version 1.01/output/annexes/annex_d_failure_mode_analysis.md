# Annex D: Failure-Mode Analysis

**What this annex adds.** Annex D carries the per-criterion and per-country failure breakdown at site–SMR pair level. Chapter 4 §4.4 summarises the dominant exclusion drivers at site level and the Chapter 5 consolidated failure section names individual countries without a viable candidate. Annex D does not repeat either view. It gives the full pair-level detail, the compound-failure distribution, and the hard-expression versus safety-floor split that the main body does not reproduce.

## Population and survivorship

The analysis covers the entire project universe, not just the shortlist. Each row in the pair-level audit corresponds to one (site, SMR design) pairing. The rubric contains eight SMR designs, so the universe is 363 sites × 8 designs = 2,904 pairs. Of these, 208 pairs survive every exclusionary gate and every safety floor under the current rubric. Survivors are the population on which composite ranking and the sensitivity analysis in Annex C operate; the remaining 2,696 pairs are excluded before any ranking conclusion is stated.

| Outcome | Pairs | Share of universe |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Survived every gate | 208 | 7.2 % |
| Failed any gate | 2,696 | 92.8 % |
| Universe | 2,904 | 100.0 % |

Safety-floor activity dominates the exclusion. About 91 per cent of failed pairs are caught by the safety floor, either alone or together with a hard-expression trigger on the same criterion.

The counts below are unique pair counts. A pair that fails the same criterion by both the hard expression and the safety floor is counted once in the `Hard and floor` column, not twice. | NH-04 | Geotechnical, slope stability | 24 | 1,288 | 0 | 1,312 | 48.7 % |
| NH-05 | Subsidence, karst, mining, oil and gas | 800 | 824 | 792 | 832 | 30.9 % |

The pair-level view gives the same headline ranking as the site-level view in Chapter 4 §4.4 but makes the mechanism visible. Emergency-planning feasibility is both the most frequent and the most floor-dominated failure (almost 80 per cent of rejections and a 9:1 floor-to-hard ratio). Slope stability is the second driver and sits almost entirely in the safety floor; sites rarely cross the 25-degree hard threshold at which the hard E-code is tripped. Subsidence and karst carry the highest share of compound hard-and-floor failures because the rubric's karst descriptor and the subsidence proxy tend to co-activate in the same geological settings. Surface-rupture screening remains a significant filter. Ecological sensitivity and settlement-liquefaction together account for less than 2 per cent of the exclusion volume.

## SMR-design invariance

All exclusionary fail expressions in the current rubric are site-physics driven (capable faults, slope angle, karst, emergency-plan feasibility composite). None references the SMR design's emergency-planning zone radius, footprint, or thermal output. The screening verdict is therefore SMR-invariant by construction; every SMR design has the same 26 surviving sites and the same 7.2 per cent pair-level survival rate. SMR choice only affects the ranking phase, through weight profiles and score interactions. If future rubric iterations introduce SMR-specific exclusions, the per-design rows will diverge.

## Compound versus single-criterion failures

A pair can fail several exclusionary criteria simultaneously. The distribution below is the count of distinct criteria on which each failed pair is caught.

| Distinct criteria failed | Pairs | Share of failed |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1,128 | 41.8 % |
| 2 | 920 | 34.1 % |
| 3 | 544 | 20.2 % |
| 4 | 96 | 3.6 % |
| 5 | 8 | 0.3 % |

About 42 per cent of exclusions are single-criterion and about 58 per cent are compound. Compound exclusions matter because a single-criterion fail can sometimes be mitigated by targeted Stage 3 investigation, whereas multi-criterion fails typically signal a systemic site-suitability issue rather than a specific data gap.

## Hard-expression versus safety-floor split

The project rubric treats a criterion as "hard" when its `condition_expr` triggers (for example `nearest_fault_km < 8`) and as a "floor" when the 0–10 ranking score sits below the criterion's pass mark of 5.0. The two mechanisms can activate together (compound). For the NuScale VOYGR-6 reference case, the split across failed pairs is:

| Mechanism | Pairs |
| ---: | ---: |
| Hard expression only | 232 |
| Safety floor only | 1,288 |
| Both (hard and floor on the same criterion) | 1,176 |

The dominance of the safety-floor path confirms that most exclusions are not caused by the rubric's worst-case hard expressions. They are caused by a screening score below the minimum acceptable band on a critical criterion. This is the behaviour the dual gate is designed to produce: critical weaknesses remove a site from ranking even when no single value crosses an absolute exclusion threshold.

## Reading the annex with the main body

Chapter 4 §4.4 names the top exclusion drivers and their interpretation. The Chapter 5 consolidated failure section carries the country-level narrative for countries with no viable candidate. Annex D adds the pair-level and per-country counts, the hard-versus-floor mechanism, and the compound-failure distribution. Reviewers who disagree with any specific exclusion can trace the verdict through the mechanism column in the pair-level view, the pass-mark condition in Annex B, and the evidence row in the scoring database.
