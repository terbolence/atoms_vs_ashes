# 7. Final Remarks

This report converts a regional inventory of coal and thermal power plant sites into a sensitivity-aware shortlist for detailed site evaluation. It does so through a traceable chain of decisions: an initial site universe defined by geography and site type, a screening workflow built on structured evidence and quality-flagged data, an evaluation framework organised around IAEA safety families and non-safety implementation criteria, a scoring system that separates exclusionary gates and safety floors from composite ranking, and a 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo sensitivity analysis that tests the ranking against reasonable uncertainty in weights, scores, and thresholds.

The value of the assessment is not in any one ranking table. It is in the chain itself. A government or project sponsor reading the shortlist can see which evidence supports a site's position, which criteria dominate the result, which are robust under sensitivity testing, and which require measurements that only Stage 3 characterisation can produce. A screening study that does not carry those four properties cannot support a defensible decision to progress a site. The chain is the product.

The scope remains narrow and deliberate. The report supports IAEA SSG-35 Stage 1 and Stage 2 decisions only: site survey, screening, comparison, ranking, and selection of sites that may justify Stage 3 characterisation. It does not replace a licence application, a site characterisation report, a vendor selection study, a procurement recommendation, or a legal opinion. Where the analysis recommends a site for progression, that recommendation means support for a decision to commission detailed characterisation, not approval to build.

Governments and operators should read the shortlist as a prioritisation instrument. A high ranking identifies a site whose screening evidence and sensitivity profile justify a Stage 3 work plan at tractable cost. It does not identify a "best" site in any regulatory or commercial sense, nor does it settle any question about public acceptance, financing, grid capacity, cooling water rights, environmental permitting, or host-country regulator readiness. Those questions are answered in Stage 3 and beyond, and they can reorder the top ten or remove a site entirely. The shortlist narrows the question; it does not answer it.

Uncertainty is part of the result. Average criterion coverage across the scored dataset is in the low-to-middle fractional range and every composite score carries a low aggregate confidence label. That does not invalidate the ranking; it defines its correct use. The Monte Carlo band around each composite score is the report's explicit statement of how much the ranking depends on unmeasured or weakly measured evidence. Stage 3 characterisation reduces that band by converting screening proxies into site-specific measurements, regulator-acceptable deliverables, and stakeholder-tested assumptions. Until that work is commissioned, the bands are the honest frame within which the ranking should be read.

The methodological contribution is reproducibility. Every ranking, sensitivity band, exclusionary verdict, and failure mode can be retraced to the source datasets, the criterion rubrics, the scoring specifications, the exclusionary rules, the sensitivity parameters, and the assumption register carried in the annexes. A reviewer who disagrees with any individual judgement can replace the inputs and rerun the chain. That is not an incidental feature of the work; it is the mechanism that makes the screening verdicts defensible at screening grade and that makes the Stage 3 priorities traceable to specific screening evidence rather than to unexplained analyst preference.

The last word belongs to the reader. This report does not select sites; it offers the client government a defensible starting set and a defensible sequence for investigating it further. Whether any site on the shortlist proceeds to detailed characterisation, and whether any site that proceeds is then found suitable for construction, remains a decision for national authorities, operators, regulators, and the public, under national law and practice, and with access to evidence that a screening study cannot reach. The work ends here and Stage 3 begins.
