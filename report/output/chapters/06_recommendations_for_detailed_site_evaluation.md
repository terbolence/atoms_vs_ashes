# 6. Recommendations for Detailed Site Evaluation

This chapter translates the screening findings into the Stage 3 work package. It describes the investigations, data gaps, regulatory engagements, and sequencing that a government or project sponsor should commission to convert a shortlisted site from a sensitivity-aware ranking outcome into a decision-quality site dossier. It does not repeat the criterion definitions in Chapter 3, the shortlist in §4.3, or the per-site residual-risk registers in Chapter 5; it draws on all three.

## 6.1 Recommended Stage 3 Investigations

Stage 3 characterisation is not a more detailed version of the Stage 1 and Stage 2 screening. It is a different kind of work. Screening tests whether a site is worth investigating under open-source evidence, project rubrics, and proxy datasets. Characterisation collects site-specific measurements, model predictions, and regulator-facing deliverables under the methodology that a national nuclear safety authority will accept. The subsections below describe the delta from screening evidence to characterisation evidence, grouped by investigation family and anchored to the shortlist in §4.3.

### Geotechnical and seismic characterisation

Screening-grade seismic evidence for the shortlist is drawn from European Seismic Hazard Model grids, a mapped capable-fault inventory, and a 760 m/s reference Vs30. This supports comparison, not design. The Stage 3 deliverable is a site-specific probabilistic seismic hazard assessment (PSHA) with epistemic logic trees, a deterministic SSE-level evaluation for the design envelope, a capable-fault investigation covering the nearest active structures, a seismic reflection or refraction profile to depth of engineering interest, and a Vs30 and dynamic-soils programme with downhole or crosshole measurements at the foundation footprint. For sites where screening geotechnical scores depend on proxy bearing capacity and depth-to-bedrock grids, this extends to boreholes, CPT or SPT coverage, laboratory tests for liquefaction triggering, and slope-stability analysis replacing the digital-surface-model artefacts flagged across the shortlist.

### Flooding, cooling water, and hydrology

Flooding at screening resolution is a pass/fail based on coarse hazard class and elevation. Stage 3 requires a design-basis flood analysis covering riverine, pluvial, coastal, surge, and dam-break contributions, with a combined-event treatment and a climate-sensitivity scenario. It requires measured river flows at the intake reach, low-flow recurrence curves for the cooling circuit, an ultimate-heat-sink availability analysis, and a thermal-discharge compliance envelope against the receiving-water standards in force. Groundwater characterisation adds an aquifer conceptual model, head monitoring, recharge estimates, and a contaminant-pathway scoping exercise. Where the shortlist contains sites with missing `nearest_river_flow_m3s` values, Stage 3 must replace the proxy band with a measured discharge before any dispersion or permitting conclusion is stated.

### Meteorology and combined natural hazards

The screening dataset uses ERA5-reanalysis statistics for wind, temperature, precipitation, and stability class. Stage 3 requires at least one year of on-site meteorological tower data (two years preferred), a severe-weather climatology built from national station records, a design wind pressure calculation, a design rainfall intensity curve, a snow-load envelope, a wildfire hazard analysis where land cover and fuel-break distance require it, and a combined-hazard screening that tests plausible concurrent events. For several shortlisted sites the screening extreme-precipitation reading is a known grid artefact; the Stage 3 meteorological pack resolves this.

### Human-induced hazards and nuclear security

Screening-grade evidence for aircraft, industrial, toxic-gas, transport, and military hazards comes from public registers, OpenStreetMap, and the GEM-class infrastructure layer. That is adequate to flag candidates, not to close them out. Stage 3 investigations for human-induced hazards are a national industrial-register survey against Seveso and comparable cadastres, a hazmat-route analysis covering rail, road, and pipeline corridors, a dedicated aviation study for low-altitude activity and controlled airspace, and a military-installation engagement with the relevant defence ministry. Nuclear security is a separate track: it covers physical protection design basis threat, cyber-security architecture, and insider-threat programme design, and it is a regulator-facing activity rather than an open-data one.

### Radiological impact and atmospheric dispersion

Screening atmospheric-dispersion scores are derived from reanalysis wind, mixing-height, and stability-class statistics; screening population is taken from global population grids and projected linearly. Stage 3 requires a site-specific atmospheric-dispersion model calibrated against on-site meteorology, a normal-operation dose envelope, a design-basis accident dose envelope, a liquid-effluent dilution and receptor study, and a population projection built from national census and municipal planning data. Groundwater and surface-water radiological pathways need explicit receptor modelling where any local user (municipal intake, agricultural withdrawal, recreational use) sits within the screening envelope.

### Emergency planning

Emergency-planning scores at screening resolution use road density, hospitals, prisons, care homes, and terrain barriers inside the emergency planning zone. This is a feasibility signal, not an emergency plan. Stage 3 requires an evacuation time estimate under summer and winter loadings, a sheltering and potassium-iodide distribution plan, a concept of operations for special populations, an agreement framework with county and national civil-protection authorities, and an exercise programme integrated with the national nuclear emergency plan. For shortlisted sites where EP-02 is the weakest ranking criterion, the evacuation time estimate is the first deliverable that can confirm or falsify the screening verdict.

### Grid and transmission

Screening grid evidence uses substation and high-voltage-line distance, line voltage, and a nominal export-capacity figure inherited from the site's existing thermal assignment. Stage 3 requires a transmission system operator load-flow and n-1 contingency study against the reference deployment envelope, a grid-connection offer covering short-circuit contribution, reactive-power capability, frequency support, and black-start participation, and an interconnection-cost estimate. For the shortlist, the dominant question is whether the 110 kV or 220 kV legacy connection must be upgraded to 400 kV, which is a programme-schedule item, not a screening flag.

### Heavy-haul, transport, and construction logistics

Screening transport scores use rail and road distance and a heavy-haul-capable flag. Stage 3 requires a swept-path analysis for abnormal-load routing, a bridge-loading and tunnel-clearance review, a port or rail-siding feasibility study where modular delivery requires it, a construction-laydown layout, and a workforce-mobilisation plan. The shortlisted sites differ materially on this axis: sites with direct rail access and a short road-from-port corridor require substantially less logistics characterisation than sites that depend on multi-bridge road-only corridors.

### Land, ownership, environment, and planning

Land availability at screening resolution is built from CORINE land cover, buildable-patch geometry, and a Natura 2000 and protected-area proximity calculation. Stage 3 requires a title search on the exact plot, a zoning and master-plan review at municipal and county level, an environmental impact assessment scoping exercise, an appropriate assessment under Habitats Directive Article 6(3) for sites inside a Natura 2000 buffer, a contaminated-land survey covering legacy coal combustion, ash lagoons, and fuel storage, and a cultural-heritage and archaeology screening. Ownership is an evidence matter: the screening profiles list parent and operating companies from structured inventories; Stage 3 confirms title, encumbrances, and any site-control pathway that depends on a state-to-state or state-to-utility transaction.

### Stakeholder and regulatory mobilisation

Stage 3 is the point at which a project becomes visible to the regulator, the grid operator, the local authority, and the public. Screening outputs do not replace any of these conversations. The deliverables are a regulator engagement memo tied to the national safety authority's pre-application guidance, a grid-connection request submitted to the transmission system operator, a local-authority information pack covering siting, employment, and infrastructure impacts, an emergency-planning coordination note with county civil-protection authorities, and a communications strategy for early public and civil-society engagement.

## 6.2 Data Gaps Requiring Field Confirmation

The shortlist in §4.3 inherits an average criterion coverage in the low-to-middle fractional range, with a low aggregate confidence label on every composite score. Not every missing criterion matters equally. The data gaps below are organised by the decision they could change, because that is how a Stage 3 work programme should be scoped. A detailed line-item register is carried in Annex E.

### Gaps that could change an exclusion outcome

A small number of screening checks can flip from pass to exclusion when a measured value replaces a grid value. The priorities are capable-fault mapping within the 5 km screening radius, liquefaction triggering under the site-specific groundwater regime, design-basis flood elevation against the measured river and basin geometry, and the military-installation classification for features inside the 25 km search radius. For one shortlisted site, the nearest military feature sits inside 3 km and is screened as unclassified open-source metadata; that entry cannot be settled without defence-ministry engagement.

### Gaps that could change a safety-floor outcome

Safety-floor thresholds remove a site from ranking when a critical criterion scores below the floor. For the shortlist the most common such criteria are evacuation-route density, ecological-sensitivity in a Natura 2000 buffer, and atmospheric-dispersion in low-wind stable-class regimes. A Stage 3 evacuation time estimate, an appropriate-assessment outcome for Natura 2000, and a site-specific dispersion model calibrated against on-site meteorology are the three deliverables most likely to move a site across the floor in either direction.

### Gaps that could change a ranking outcome

Most unmeasured criteria do not change exclusion or floor status; they tighten the Monte Carlo band around the composite. Four clusters dominate this category across the shortlist: industrial, toxic-gas, transport, and external-fire hazards (HI-02 to HI-05) where the absence of a positive feature in open data collapses to a pass-mark default, grid-connection voltage and firm-capacity headroom (NS-02), existing-infrastructure reuse (NS-06), and non-radiological environmental impact (NS-07). Converting these from pass-mark defaults to measured values raises the lower Monte Carlo bracket and can reorder the top ten without changing who is in or out.

## 6.3 Recommended Regulatory and Stakeholder Follow-Up

Stage 3 is not a licensing stage. It is the point at which a candidate site becomes visible to the authorities whose approval is required before a licence application can be filed, and the point at which those authorities first need a site-specific evidence file. The engagements below are sequenced so that each produces usable output for the next.

### National nuclear regulator

An initial pre-application memo should describe the reference deployment envelope, the shortlisted site, the Stage 1 and Stage 2 screening outcome, the Stage 3 work plan, and the timeline for a potential construction-licence application. The memo asks the regulator to identify the expected safety-case structure, the national interpretation of IAEA site-evaluation requirements, and the regulator's position on project-specific questions such as design-basis earthquake methodology, design-basis flood aggregation, and atmospheric-dispersion modelling tools. This engagement is the reference point against which all later Stage 3 deliverables are assembled.

### Transmission system operator

The transmission system operator owns the grid-connection question. A formal connection request triggers the load-flow, short-circuit, and n-1 contingency studies that determine whether the legacy thermal connection carries the new reference-case output, whether the substation and line voltage must be upgraded, and what the firm-capacity headroom looks like. For several shortlisted sites the current 110 kV or 220 kV connection is adequate for screening but inadequate for a 462 MWe NuScale VOYGR-6 (6 × 77 MWe modules) plant; the transmission system operator's answer, not the screening score, determines the connection topology in the project schedule.

### Water authority and basin regulator

Cooling-water abstraction rights, thermal-discharge limits, and low-flow recurrence curves are set by the national or regional water authority. A Stage 3 engagement submits the reference-case water demand, the cooling-circuit design choice, the discharge temperature and flow envelope, and the basin context, and asks the authority to confirm abstraction permitting, discharge permitting, and drought-management obligations under the Water Framework Directive. Where the screening evidence carries a missing river-flow measurement, this engagement is the route by which the missing value is recovered.

### Local authority, zoning, and planning

Municipal and county authorities control zoning, master-plan integration, cultural-heritage screening, and local permitting. A Stage 3 local-authority pack should describe the project footprint, the construction phase, the operational phase, the employment and tax context, the transport impact, and the buffer-zone implications. It should request a zoning review, a master-plan compatibility statement, and an early indication of the municipal position on the project.

### Emergency-planning authorities

National civil-protection authorities and county inspectorates coordinate the emergency planning zone, the urgent protective-action zone, and the sheltering and evacuation plans. A Stage 3 engagement aligns the reference-case source-term envelope, the EPZ and UPZ radii, the evacuation time estimate, and the special-population interfaces with the national nuclear emergency plan. Where a shortlisted site has dense special populations or a constrained road network inside the EPZ, this engagement is the first place that the screening EP score is tested against planning reality.

### Land owner and operator

Where the shortlisted site is on a state-owned or utility-owned plot, land control is an administrative question rather than a title-search question. Where the plot is privately held or fragmented, a Stage 3 engagement confirms title, encumbrances, and the negotiation pathway to site control. The ownership and parent-company information in the Chapter 5 site profiles is a starting point, not a title record.

### Civil society and public

Early public engagement is a national-practice question rather than a technical one. A Stage 3 communications strategy should be designed in coordination with the client government and local authority, should be timed to the pre-application stage rather than to licence submission, and should provide straightforward answers to the four DOE-identified public-interest questions on plant size, siting on or near the original plant, infrastructure reuse, and the retirement-to-operation gap [4].

## 6.4 Prioritisation of Next-Step Work

Stage 3 characterisation is expensive. A prioritised work plan is the difference between delivering a decision-quality dossier in three to five years and losing two years to sequencing errors. The sequence below translates the §4.3 shortlist into a four-stage action plan with a decision gate between each stage.

### Stage A: immediate desk-data validation (weeks 1 to 12)

For the top six shortlisted sites, run a structured data-validation pass against national sources that the screening dataset did not use: national seismic catalogues, national flood maps, national water-authority hydrology, transmission system operator published interconnection data, municipal and county zoning maps, Seveso and comparable industrial registers, and defence-ministry airspace publications. The output is a single validated screening file per site, carrying national-authority-grade evidence where it exists and an explicit gap list where it does not. Decision gate: confirm that no previously unseen exclusionary condition has emerged before committing field-investigation funds.

### Stage B: targeted field and archival work (months 3 to 18)

For sites that pass Stage A, commission the high-impact field investigations: a geotechnical borehole programme at the foundation footprint, a meteorological tower for at least one year, a site-specific water-flow gauging campaign where the intake reach is ungauged, a capable-fault ground-truth survey within the 5 km radius, and a contaminated-land survey covering legacy coal-combustion and ash-lagoon footprints. Pair these with the archival pass: title search, zoning review, EIA and appropriate-assessment scoping, and the ministry-level engagement on human-induced hazards where it applies. Decision gate: confirm that the measured values support the screening ranking before commissioning the full Stage 3 safety-case build.

### Stage C: country and site due diligence (months 6 to 24)

Run the regulatory and stakeholder engagements described in §6.3 in parallel with Stage B. Open the regulator pre-application dialogue, lodge the transmission system operator connection request, brief the local authority, brief the civil-protection authority, and open the defence-ministry engagement where a human-induced hazard sits inside the buffer. The output is a regulator-acceptable scope of work for the Stage 3 safety case, a signed grid-connection path, a local-authority zoning statement, and an agreed emergency-planning coordination framework. Decision gate: confirm that no regulatory or stakeholder constraint removes the site from contention before committing to the Stage 3 safety case.

### Stage D: decision to commission Stage 3 characterisation (month 18 onward)

The final gate is the decision to commission the full Stage 3 safety case. By this point the site has a validated screening file, measured values for the highest-impact criteria, a regulator-acceptable scope, a grid-connection path, a local-authority position, and a civil-protection framework. The Stage 3 safety case then delivers the PSHA, the design-basis flood analysis, the combined-hazards treatment, the dispersion and dose envelope, the full EIA, the emergency plan, and the integrated site characterisation report that supports a construction-licence application. Anything the client wishes to do at this point is conditional on the Stage 3 safety-case outcome, not on the ranking in §4.3.
