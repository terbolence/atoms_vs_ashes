# Negotino power station Site Profile

Negotino power station is a coal/thermal site in North Macedonia that has been tested against the NuScale VOYGR-6 reference deployment envelope. This profile reads the screening evidence at a level that supports a decision to progress toward Stage 3 characterisation. It is not a site-suitability determination, vendor recommendation, or licensing finding.

### Interpretation - Natural Hazards (NH)


Natural hazards at Negotino are materially easier than at Oslomej and slightly easier than Bitola on ground motion, but they still need site-specific confirmation. Seismic: Ground Motion (NH-01) records 0.219 g at the 475-year return period and 0.481 g at the 2,475-year return period, just below the project's Phase 2 avoidance trigger. Surface rupture is not the binding issue: the nearest mapped capable fault is 17.7 km away and the recorded slip rate is 0.224 mm/yr for MKCF00B. The site has moderate liquefaction susceptibility on loam, bearing capacity of 86.0 kPa, and 19.7 m depth to bedrock. Topography is more constrained than Bitola, with a 7.63 degree site slope and an 83.2 degree maximum slope within the 1 km box, but the mean slope remains within the pass-mark band. River flooding is negligible in the screening file, the coast is 116.9 km away, the screening wind-speed index is 7.38 m/s, and the volcanic hazard class is negligible. Stage 3 should confirm the sub-threshold ground-motion result with site-specific PSHA, verify the Vardar and Negotinska Reka flood envelope, and complete a topographic and geotechnical campaign across the usable development area.

### Interpretation - Human-Induced and Security-Relevant Hazards (HI)


Human-induced hazards at Negotino are dominated by the Krivolak military and aviation setting. Military Installations (HI-06) scores 1.5/10: the nearest military feature is 6.96 km away and the bundle names the Krivolak Training Area. Aircraft Crash (HI-01) is also an avoidance issue because Krivolak Airstrip is 6.34 km from the site and the nearest flight-path proxy is 3.17 km away. Those two findings should be treated together because the airstrip and military feature form a single operational context for Stage 3 screening. Electromagnetic Interference (HI-07) is less severe than at Bitola or Oslomej, with the nearest transmitter 4.5 km away and seven transmitters in the search radius. Industrial Explosions (HI-02), Toxic/Gas Releases (HI-03), and External Fires (HI-04) each score 9.5/10 in the frozen run, but the report should still treat those as screening-grade findings until a national hazardous-neighbour cadastre is reviewed. Stage 3 should begin with defence-interface engagement for Krivolak, then complete an aircraft-crash assessment and an updated industrial and transport-hazard inventory.

### Interpretation - Radiological Impact and Emergency Planning (RI / EP)


Radiological impact and emergency-planning conditions at Negotino are acceptable for continued screening, but road capacity is a clear weakness. Emergency Planning Feasibility (EP-01) records a 61.8/100 composite and is marked feasible, with strong geography and special-population sub-scores but a road sub-score of 37.3/100. Evacuation Routes (EP-02) therefore remains the family constraint: road density in the EPZ is 0.273 km/km<sup>2</sup>, with 536.1 km of road and motorway access recorded. Population density is higher close to the site than at Bitola, at 202.8 people/km<sup>2</sup> within 5 km, but falls to 37.3 people/km<sup>2</sup> within 25 km; the 25 km population is 73,257 people and the 60-year projection is 49,159. Groundwater dispersion is relatively favourable on a low-permeability aquifer, while Atmospheric Dispersion (RI-01) remains unscored despite 0.6 m/s annual mean wind speed, 580.5 m mixing height and NW prevailing wind direction. Stage 3 should model EPZ clearance, confirm meteorology, and translate the feasible EP composite into an operational emergency plan for the hospital and prison inventory inside the EPZ.

### Interpretation - Non-Safety and Implementation Considerations (NS)


Non-safety and implementation conditions at Negotino are mixed: the water and wider land envelope are useful, but the grid and current development-area values create a major qualification. Cooling Water Availability (NS-01) is strong, with a major-river source 2.26 km from the site, 163.7 m<sup>3</sup>/s flow, and a Low-Medium water-stress label. The site snapshot records 192.9 ha of available surface area, but only 5.2 ha of current development-area evidence, so Stage 3 must reconcile the wider surface-area figure with actual contiguous, controlled and buildable land. Grid Connection (NS-02) is the binding implementation issue: the nearest substation is 0.61 km away and the nearest high-voltage line is 2.1 km away, but the highest nearby voltage is 110 kV and inherited export capacity is 165 MW. That is below the 462 MWe reference envelope and explains the grid avoidance flag. Ecological Sensitivity (NS-08) also needs field confirmation because the nearest protected area is 4.903 km away and the data quality is insufficient. Stage 3 should first scope a voltage and export-capacity pathway, then verify land control and contiguity beyond the 5.2 ha development estimate.

Baseline composite score is 6.707, bracketed by Monte Carlo at 5.846-7.171. National stability band is `H` with a top-10% hit rate of 0% across 12 scored Monte Carlo scenarios.

Negotino's baseline composite score is 6.707, with a Monte Carlo bracket of 5.846-7.171. The point estimate is close to Bitola and the score bands overlap, so the rank difference should not be over-read. The national sensitivity result is the decisive distinction: Negotino is in stability band H and records a 0% national top-10 hit rate across the 12 scored national sensitivity scenarios. That means it is a plausible second site in the baseline ranking, but not a robust alternative leader. The composite benefits from water availability, population and several natural-hazard scores, while grid connection, military proximity, aircraft-crash screening, evacuation routes, EMI and land certainty remain the practical constraints. Stage 3 should only advance Negotino after a focused transmission, land and defence-interface review shows that the avoidance flags can be retired without losing the brownfield advantage.
