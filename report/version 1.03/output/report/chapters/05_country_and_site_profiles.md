# 5. Country and Site Profiles

Country and site profiles translate the Stage 1-2 screening results into national decision packets. Each country profile starts with the full national distribution of assessed coal and thermal sites, then separates full-pass candidates, avoidance-flagged sites, and hard-failed sites before naming a preferred lead. That order matters: it prevents a high-scoring site from being read in isolation and keeps the evidence base visible for ministries that may need a portfolio view rather than a single-site decision.

The published Chapter 5 set now covers the accepted country packets for Austria, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Bulgaria, Czechia, Croatia, Hungary, Latvia, Moldova, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Poland, Romania, Serbia, Slovakia, Türkiye, and Ukraine. Romania and Turceni established the first accepted packet, but the chapter is no longer a Romania-only surface. The underlying claim boundary remains unchanged throughout the chapter. A site can be recommended for detailed Stage 3 characterisation, but no profile is a site-suitability determination, licensing finding, procurement recommendation, or vendor-specific approval.

## 5.1 Country Profile Structure and Interpretation Rules

Each country profile follows a consistent evidence sequence. It opens with the national count of ranked sites and the split between full-pass, avoidance-flagged, and hard-failed records. The leading site is introduced only after that distribution, together with its national composite score, Monte Carlo interval, stability band, and top-tier hit rate from the project's 50,000-iteration national sensitivity analysis.

The ranked country ledger is the core evidence surface. It reports national rank, site name, screening class, composite score, Monte Carlo bracket, national stability band, top-tier hit rate, and evidence coverage. Avoidance and hard-fail Pareto sections then identify which criteria control the country's next-step work. This makes the profile useful both for selecting near-term Stage 3 candidates and for understanding what would be required to unlock a wider brownfield pool.

Countries with no viable NuScale VOYGR-6 candidate are not forced into the same profile shape. They are routed to a consolidated failure section that records failed criteria, threshold distances where measurable, and whether the constraint appears structural, costly to mitigate, or data-limited. Countries outside the current published roster are omitted from the country-profile sequence.

## 5.3 Site-Level Summaries and Supporting Maps

Selected site profiles use the same structure as the accepted Turceni profile. Each profile begins with a snapshot table covering location, installed thermal capacity, available surface area, available surface area for development, national score, stability band, and rank. If either surface-area value is missing from first-party project data, the profile states `N/A` rather than inferring a number.

The body of each site profile separates evidence by criterion family: natural hazards, human-induced and security-relevant hazards, radiological impact and emergency planning, and non-safety implementation considerations. Family sections show measured evidence first, then interpretation. The residual-risk register and Stage 3 follow-up checklist are written as work planning surfaces for field confirmation, not as conclusions that a site is suitable or licensable.

Meteorological rows in the site profiles use screening indices for precipitation and wind exposure. These values support relative ranking only. Where the index is physically implausible as a climatological measurement, the profile should read it as a data-quality flag and route it to national meteorological confirmation in Stage 3.

The accepted Turceni profile is the current completed site example: [Turceni power station](05_country_and_site_profiles/sites/RO_turceni_power_station.md). Country status maps are used as the primary geographic summary, while site charts show criterion scores and family contributions where generated figures exist.

## 5.4 Ownership, Infrastructure, and Coal-to-Nuclear Interpretation

Ownership and infrastructure language is factual first. A profile may report parent entities, immediate operator information, generating units, retired capacity, cooling source proximity, grid connection evidence, rail access, and land-area indicators where those values exist in the local bundle. It must not imply land control, public acceptance, procurement readiness, financing availability, or regulatory acceptance.

Coal-to-nuclear interpretation is therefore framed as Stage 1-2 opportunity screening. Brownfield assets can shorten later investigation by preserving grid, cooling, transport, industrial-zoning and workforce context, but those attributes do not remove the need for site-specific Stage 3 work. The profile's role is to identify where that work would be most productively directed.

## 5.5 National Sensitivity, Rank Stability, and Stage 3 Sequencing

Country and site stability statements use the national sensitivity analysis only. The relevant question is whether a site remains robust within its own country's candidate set under weight perturbation, not whether it outperforms sites in a regional pool. The profile therefore treats national band, top-tier hit rate, and score interval as sequencing evidence for Stage 3 investigation.

High stability supports prioritisation, not approval. A stable full-pass site is a defensible first candidate for detailed characterisation; an avoidance-flagged site may remain valuable if the controlling issue is measurable and potentially resolvable; a hard-failed site remains a comparator unless the underlying measurement is refuted or the governing threshold changes. This keeps Chapter 5 aligned with IAEA SSG-35 Stage 1 and Stage 2 screening boundaries.
