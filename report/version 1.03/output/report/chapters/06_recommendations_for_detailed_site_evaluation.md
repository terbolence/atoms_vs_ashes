# 6. Recommendations for Detailed Site Evaluation

The siting framework used in this report follows the three progressive stages in IAEA SSG-35 (IAEA, 2015), supported by IAEA SSR-1 site-evaluation safety requirements and the EPRI siting guidance (EPRI, 2022). Each stage is defined briefly below in the order the norms apply them, so that the scope of this chapter and the work that has to follow it are unambiguous.

**Stage 1 — Site Survey.** Regional or national review that identifies the candidate areas and screens them against high-level exclusionary criteria. The output is a screened inventory of locations that are worth comparative evaluation. In this report Stage 1 is presented in Chapter 2 and the outputs feed the Stage 2 candidate pool.

**Stage 2 — Site Selection.** Comparative evaluation of the surviving Stage 1 candidates against the project's criterion rubric. The output is one or more sites recommended for detailed evaluation, together with the evidence base, the criterion-level scores, the national rank-stability picture and the residual-risk register that the next stage has to resolve. Selection at this stage is driven by the composite ranking together with socioeconomic-and-implementation considerations (grid interface, land context, cooling, workforce and host-community readiness), and remains a screening-grade recommendation pending Stage 3 confirmation. In this report Stage 2 is presented in Chapter 3 (method), Chapter 4 (regional results) and Chapter 5 (per-country and per-site profiles).

**Stage 3 — Site Evaluation and Confirmation.** Detailed site-specific characterisation, regulator-facing analyses and design-confirmation work against the host-country requirements. Stage 3 collects the site-specific measurements, authority engagements and design-basis evaluations that take a recommended site forward into a licence-quality decision basis.

**Scope of this report.** This report performs Stages 1 and 2 in support of an ulterior Stage 3 that the host country, regulator or project sponsor would commission for any site they wish to take forward. The Stage 3 work programme described in this chapter is therefore a forward-looking scoping artefact: it identifies the investigations, confirmations and authority engagements that the next phase of work has to address before any shortlisted site can be treated as a candidate for licence-quality evaluation. The recommendations apply to the NuScale VOYGR-6 reference envelope only.

**Deviations from the SSG-35 stages.** The screening rubric used in Stages 1 and 2 follows the SSG-35 criterion families and the IAEA NS-G-3.x technical guidance, with two project-specific extensions documented in the methodology annex: the NH-02 capable-fault stand-off radius is set as a UI-tunable run-profile parameter (the snapshot frozen for this report uses 5 km against the SSG-9 norm default of 8 km, recorded in the per-site provenance block), and the HI-01 aircraft-crash criterion reads only the published commercial-airport catalogue, treating small general-aviation airfields and helipads as out of scope for Stage 1-2 screening. Both deviations are visible in the per-site verdict lines and the country profiles.

This chapter is written as a work-planning bridge for the Stage 3 phase that the host country or project sponsor would commission next. Chapter 3 defines the screening and ranking method, Chapter 4 fixes the cross-country results, and Chapter 5 carries the country and selected-site residual-risk registers. Where those inputs remain incomplete, this chapter records the dependency and avoids assigning named-site priorities that the accepted evidence has not yet supported.

## 6.1 Recommended Stage 3 Investigations

Stage 3 characterisation is a different evidentiary standard from Stage 1 and Stage 2 screening. Screening is designed to identify where further effort is justified. Stage 3 collects site-specific measurements, regulator-facing analyses, and authority confirmations that can support the next formal project decisions (IAEA, 2019).

### Geotechnical and seismic characterisation

Each selected site should receive a site-specific probabilistic seismic hazard assessment, a deterministic safe-shutdown-earthquake evaluation for the reference envelope, and a capable-fault investigation focused on features that could affect the nuclear island and associated safety systems. The field programme should include boreholes, cone penetration or standard penetration testing, downhole or crosshole shear-wave velocity measurements, groundwater observations, liquefaction triggering assessment, bearing-capacity tests, and slope-stability analysis. Screening-grade seismic and geotechnical scores should remain decision-support evidence until those site measurements are available.

### Flooding, cooling water, and hydrology

Selected sites require a design-basis flood analysis covering riverine, pluvial, coastal where relevant, dam-break, drainage, and combined-event pathways. Cooling-water assessment should confirm abstraction reliability, low-flow recurrence, thermal-discharge limits, ultimate heat sink availability, and drought-management obligations. Where the screening evidence lacks measured discharge, intake reach geometry, or dilution information, Stage 3 should treat those values as early field priorities before any cooling or liquid-pathway conclusion is hardened.

### Meteorology and combined natural hazards

Stage 3 should replace screening meteorology with site-specific measurements and national station records. The work programme should include at least one year of on-site meteorological observation, severe-weather climatology, design wind pressure, design rainfall, snow and ice loading, wildfire screening where land-cover and fuel-distance conditions require it, and a combined-hazards review. Sites with implausible screening precipitation or wind values need early national-record reconciliation because those values can affect drainage, dispersion, emergency planning, and design-basis assumptions.

### Human-induced hazards and nuclear security

Human-induced hazard work should cover aircraft activity, industrial hazards, hazardous material corridors, pipelines, rail and road transport, nearby high-energy infrastructure, military areas, and electromagnetic sources. The Stage 3 file should use national authority registers, operator declarations, field reconnaissance, and ministry engagement rather than treating open public evidence as conclusive. Nuclear security is a separate regulator-facing workstream covering design basis threat, physical protection, cyber security, insider-threat controls, and protected-area interfaces.

### Radiological impact and atmospheric dispersion

Radiological assessment should move from screening proxies to site-specific atmospheric, surface-water, groundwater, and population modelling. The Stage 3 deliverables should include an atmospheric dispersion model calibrated to site meteorology, normal-operation and accident dose envelopes, liquid effluent dilution analysis, groundwater pathway assessment, receptor identification, and population projections based on national census and planning evidence. These deliverables are necessary before any screened site can be described as suitable for a licence-quality radiological case.

### Emergency planning

Emergency-planning scores in this report are feasibility signals. Stage 3 should produce an evacuation time estimate, sheltering and potassium-iodide distribution concept, special-population plan, communications concept, and exercise framework aligned with the national nuclear emergency plan. Road density, healthcare access, terrain, river barriers, seasonal population, and special facilities should be tested under realistic peak and adverse-weather conditions.

### Grid, transport, and construction logistics

The grid workstream should begin with the transmission system operator and should test load-flow, short-circuit, n-1 contingency, reactive power, frequency support, black-start participation, grid reinforcement, and interconnection cost for a 462 MWe reference plant. Transport and logistics should include abnormal-load route surveys, bridge and tunnel checks, port or rail-siding feasibility where relevant, construction laydown planning, and workforce mobilisation. These issues affect schedule and cost rather than nuclear safety, but they can still determine whether a brownfield candidate is practicable.

### Land, ownership, environment, and planning

Stage 3 should verify exact plot boundaries, title, encumbrances, zoning, municipal plans, site control, and any state-to-utility or private-owner transaction path. Environmental work should include scoping for environmental impact assessment, Habitats Directive Article 6(3) review where a protected ecological receptor may be affected, contaminated-land investigation for coal-combustion legacy areas, ash and fuel-storage areas, cultural heritage screening, and land-use compatibility. Ownership and site control must be confirmed as legal facts, not inferred from operating-company or parent-entity records.

## 6.2 Data Gaps Requiring Field Confirmation

Data gaps should be prioritised by the decision they can change. A missing or weak value matters most when it can alter exclusionary status, safety-floor status, national rank stability, or the Stage 3 work scope. Annex E should carry the line-item assumption and data-limitation register once all selected-site profiles have been accepted.

| Gap class               | Possible decision effect                                                     | Stage 3 confirmation route                                                            |
| :---------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Exclusionary trigger    | Could remove a site from further consideration.                              | Field measurement, authority confirmation, and regulator-facing technical judgement.  |
| Safety-floor criterion  | Could move a site into or out of the ranked candidate set.                   | Site-specific modelling, national data review, and independent technical review.      |
| Ranking driver          | Could reorder candidates within the same country.                            | Better measured values, ownership confirmation, grid study, or environmental scoping. |
| Confidence and coverage | Could widen or narrow the national sensitivity band.                         | Evidence-quality upgrade and assumption-register closure.                             |
| Programme constraint    | Could delay or redirect Stage 3 even if safety screening remains favourable. | Grid, land, stakeholder, permitting, and operator due diligence.                      |

The most immediate field-confirmation categories are consistent across selected sites. Natural-hazard gaps usually concern capable faults, local geotechnical conditions, precipitation, flood level, cooling-water availability, and combined hazards. Human-induced hazard gaps usually concern military features, hazardous facilities, flight activity, transport corridors, and electromagnetic sources. Radiological and emergency-planning gaps usually concern dispersion inputs, population forecasts, special-population interfaces, evacuation timing, and water-pathway receptors. Non-safety gaps usually concern grid reinforcement, site control, buildable footprint, ecological receptors, construction logistics, workforce availability, and infrastructure reuse.

The site-by-site gap pattern is now carried in the Chapter 5 residual-risk registers. The recurring Stage 3 tasks are consistent across the profiles: confirm national-source evidence, replace screening proxies with field values, resolve avoidance flags before characterisation funding, and preserve country-specific caveats where security, access or institutional conditions control the practical sequence.

## 6.3 Recommended Regulatory and Stakeholder Follow-Up

Regulatory and stakeholder follow-up should begin before expensive field campaigns are fully committed. Early engagement gives the client government a chance to confirm the evidence standard expected by the national nuclear regulator, the grid operator, environmental authorities, civil-protection bodies, local authorities, land owners, and affected communities.

### National nuclear regulator

The first regulatory deliverable should be a pre-application engagement memo. It should describe the reference deployment envelope, the Stage 1 and Stage 2 screening outcome, the proposed Stage 3 scope, the safety-case structure, and the intended sequence of site investigations. The regulator should be asked to confirm expectations for seismic, flood, meteorological, radiological, emergency-planning, external-event, and environmental evidence before the sponsor commits to the full site-characterisation programme.

### Transmission system operator

The transmission system operator should receive a formal connection-enquiry package for each selected site. That package should test whether the existing thermal-site connection can support the 462 MWe reference envelope, whether voltage upgrades are required, what reinforcement is needed under n-1 contingency, and how grid-connection timing affects the Stage 3 programme. Screening distance to substations and lines is a useful first signal; the operator's system study is the decision evidence.

### Water, environmental, and planning authorities

Water authorities should confirm abstraction rights, low-flow constraints, thermal-discharge conditions, drought obligations, and river-basin management requirements. Environmental and planning authorities should confirm environmental impact assessment scoping, Habitats Directive Article 6(3) obligations where relevant, zoning compatibility, contaminated-land requirements, cultural heritage screening, and municipal planning interfaces. These engagements should be tied to the exact plot and cooling concept rather than to a generic plant description.

### Emergency-planning and civil-protection authorities

Civil-protection authorities should review the emergency planning zone concept, evacuation time estimate, sheltering strategy, special-population interfaces, communications plan, and exercise schedule. The Stage 3 question is whether the emergency plan can be made practical under national law, local road conditions, seasonal loadings, and adverse weather. Screening scores identify where the issue may arise; civil-protection engagement tests the plan against operating reality.

### Land owner, operator, and local community

Land-owner and operator engagement should confirm title, encumbrances, operating constraints, demolition or remediation obligations, ash and fuel-storage legacy issues, and any route to site control. Local engagement should start with factual information on plant size, siting on or near the existing power site, infrastructure reuse, workforce transition, and the timing gap between thermal retirement and any future nuclear project (U.S. DOE, 2024). Public engagement should be coordinated with the national client and local authority, and should avoid presenting the screening outcome as a decision to build.

## 6.4 Prioritisation of Next-Step Work

Prioritisation should follow the same logic for every selected site: validate the screening record, retire exclusionary and safety-floor uncertainty, engage authorities early, and commit to full site characterisation only after the first evidence gates are passed. This keeps Stage 3 cost proportional to the strength and stability of the Stage 1 and Stage 2 result.

| Work package                                            | Indicative timing | Decision purpose                                                                                   | Minimum output                                                                                                             |
| :------------------------------------------------------ | :---------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A. National evidence validation                         | Weeks 1-12        | Confirm that no known national source changes exclusionary or safety-floor status.                 | Validated screening file, evidence-quality upgrade list, and unresolved data-gap register.                                 |
| B. High-impact field and archival work                  | Months 3-18       | Replace the highest-risk proxies with site measurements and legal facts.                           | Geotechnical, meteorological, hydrological, title, zoning, environmental, and human-induced hazard evidence.               |
| C. Authority engagement and due diligence               | Months 6-24       | Confirm that regulator, grid, water, planning, emergency, and land-control pathways are practical. | Regulator scope response, grid pathway, water and planning position, emergency-planning framework, and land-control route. |
| D. Decision to commission full Stage 3 characterisation | Month 18 onward   | Decide whether the site merits the full licence-quality site-evaluation programme.                 | Agreed Stage 3 scope, budget, schedule, risk register, and authority engagement record.                                    |

The first work programme should separate full-pass characterisation from avoidance-flag unlock work. Full-pass sites can move directly into Stage 3 scoping once national authorities accept the screening basis. Avoidance-flagged sites should receive a narrower issue-resolution package before expensive field characterisation is commissioned.

The method can also be expanded beyond coal and thermal sites. Other large industrial sites may have grid, cooling, transport, land, and workforce characteristics relevant to SMR accommodation. They should enter the framework through the same Stage 1 and Stage 2 gates, with no relaxation of safety, emergency-planning, environmental, or security expectations.
