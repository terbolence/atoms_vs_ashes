# Document Map: IAEA SSG-9 (Rev. 1)
**Title:** Seismic Hazards in Site Evaluation for Nuclear Installations  
**Series:** IAEA Safety Standards Series No. SSG-9 (Rev. 1) (Specific Safety Guide)  
**Year:** 2022 | **Pages:** 98 | **Supersedes:** SSG-9 (2010)

---

## Purpose
Provides recommendations on evaluating seismic hazards at nuclear installation sites to meet SSR-1 Requirements 15 and 16. Covers vibratory ground motion, fault displacement, and seismically induced geological/geotechnical hazards.

---

## Section Map

### Section 1: Introduction (pp. 1–4, §1.1–1.13)
- Supersedes SSG-9 (2010). Incorporates lessons from recent strong earthquakes affecting nuclear installations.
- Applies to all nuclear installations via graded approach.
- Covers both new and existing installations.
- Bridges seismic hazard assessment and design basis determination.

### Section 2: General Aspects of Seismic Hazard Assessment (pp. 5–8, §2.1–2.11)
- Reproduces SSR-1 Requirements 1, 15, and 16.
- Both PSHA and DSHA methods should be considered.
- Uncertainties (epistemic and aleatory) must be addressed.
- Expert judgement and structured expert elicitation processes recommended.

### Section 3: Database of Information and Investigations (pp. 9–24, §3.1–3.59)
- **General (§3.1–3.15):** Data collection at regional (150–300 km), near-regional (25 km), site vicinity (5 km), and site area scales. Multi-disciplinary approach.
- **Geological/geophysical/geotechnical database (§3.16–3.35):** Regional geological mapping, tectonic models, remote sensing, field investigations, subsurface characterization.
- **Seismological database (§3.36–3.59):** Earthquake catalogues (historical + instrumental), magnitude homogenization, completeness analysis, focal mechanisms, stress field characterization, strong motion recordings.

### Section 4: Development of Seismic Source Models (pp. 25–31, §4.1–4.30)
- **General (§4.1–4.8):** Identify and characterize all seismic sources. Use multiple interpretations to capture epistemic uncertainty.
- **Seismogenic structures (§4.9–4.20):** Identified fault sources — geometry, slip rate, maximum magnitude, recurrence.
- **Zones of diffuse seismicity (§4.21–4.30):** Areal source zones for distributed seismicity.

### Section 5: Methods for Estimating Ground Motion (pp. 32–37, §5.1–5.23)
- **Ground motion prediction equations (§5.6–5.16):** Selection, testing, adjustment to site conditions.
- **Simulation methods (§5.17–5.23):** Physics-based simulation as complement to empirical GMPEs.

### Section 6: Vibratory Ground Motion Hazard Analysis (pp. 38–47, §6.1–6.24)
- **PSHA (§6.8–6.14):** Probabilistic seismic hazard analysis — logic tree, hazard curves, deaggregation, uncertainty quantification.
- **DSHA (§6.15–6.18):** Deterministic analysis — controlling earthquake, conservative approach.
- **Site response analysis (§6.19–6.24):** Soil effects, amplification, site-specific spectra.

### Section 7: Fault Displacement (pp. 48–54, §7.1–7.18)
- **Capable fault definition (§7.3–7.9):** Fault that has shown movement within a defined timeframe and is significant to safety.
- **New sites (§7.10–7.11):** Alternative site required if capable fault affects safety.
- **Existing sites (§7.12–7.18):** Re-evaluation, probabilistic fault displacement hazard analysis.

### Section 8: Parameters for Design (pp. 55–61, §8.1–8.30)
- **Ground motion parameters (§8.1–8.17):** SL-1 and SL-2 levels, response spectra, time histories, PGA.
- **Fault displacement parameters (§8.18).**
- **Other seismic hazards (§8.19–8.30):** Liquefaction, slope instability, subsidence, seismically induced flooding/fire.

### Section 9: Non-NPP Installations (pp. 62–65, §9.1–9.13)
- Graded approach: screening → categorization → reduced scope analysis.

### Section 10: Management System (pp. 66–70, §10.1–10.19)
- Project management, peer review, documentation requirements.

### Annex: Typical PSHA Output (pp. 73–76)
- Example hazard curves, uniform hazard spectra, deaggregation plots.

## Key Siting Criteria Extracted
- **Capable fault screening distance:** 5 km site vicinity (minimum), broader for regional assessment
- **Regional investigation radius:** 150–300 km depending on seismotectonic setting
- **PGA/spectral acceleration:** Compared against NuScale design envelope
- **Return period:** 10⁻⁴/year for design basis (typical for NPPs)
