# Document Map: IAEA SSG-35
**Title:** Site Survey and Site Selection for Nuclear Installations  
**Series:** IAEA Safety Standards Series No. SSG-35 (Specific Safety Guide)  
**Year:** 2015 | **Pages:** 84 | **Supersedes:** 50-SG-S9 (1984)

---

## Purpose
Primary procedural framework for the siting process: covers site survey, screening, candidate site selection, and ranking for nuclear installations. Supplements NS-R-3 (now SSR-1) requirements.

---

## Section Map

### Section 1: Introduction (pp. 1–5, §1.1–1.18)
- **Background (§1.1–1.9):** Establishes SSG-35 within the IAEA safety framework. Defence in depth (SF-1 Principle 8) requires adequate site selection. Siting process affects costs, public acceptance, and safety. Poor planning can cause major delays.
- **Objective (§1.10–1.11):** Provide recommendations for criteria and approaches to identify suitable nuclear installation sites. Intended for government bodies, future licensees, and contractors.
- **Scope (§1.12–1.17):** Addresses safety considerations in siting. Covers both new sites and new installations at existing sites. Applies graded approach for non-NPP installations. Does NOT cover site characterization or licensing.
- **Structure (§1.18):** Overview of sections 2–7 and Appendix/Annexes.

### Section 2: General Description of Siting and Site Evaluation Process (pp. 6–9, §2.1–2.9)
- **Five stages defined (§2.1):** Site Survey → Site Selection → Site Characterization → Pre-operational → Operational
- **Siting process = Stages 1+2 (§2.3):** Site survey identifies candidate sites from large regions; site selection screens and ranks them.
- **Site evaluation starts at Stage 2 (§2.4–2.5):** Overlaps with siting; continues through entire plant lifetime.
- **Key Figure:** Fig. 1 — Stages in siting and site evaluation process; Fig. 2 — Outcomes of each stage (SER, PSAR, FSAR, PSR).
- **Three important pre-construction inputs (§2.7):** (a) site suitability confirmation, (b) site design basis parameters, (c) PSAR preparation.

### Section 3: General Recommendations for the Siting Process (pp. 10–19, §3.1–3.27)
- **Three-step process (§3.3):**
  1. Regional analysis → identify potential sites
  2. Screening → exclusion + discretionary criteria → candidate sites
  3. Evaluation, comparison, ranking → preferred sites
- **Siting criteria types (§3.6–3.10):** Regional criteria, screening criteria (exclusionary + discretionary), ranking criteria. All elaborated in Table I-1 (Annex I).
- **Exclusion criteria basis (§3.11–3.13):** For negative site attributes where no engineering solution exists. Existence of hazard alone not sufficient basis for exclusion.
- **Discretionary criteria (§3.14):** Used iteratively to increase/decrease candidate pool.
- **Specific screening criteria (§3.18):** Reproduces key NS-R-3 requirements: population radiological exposure (§2.27), capable faults (§3.7), collapse/subsidence (§3.35–3.36), liquefaction (§3.38–3.40), aircraft crashes (§3.44–3.47), chemical explosions (§3.49–3.50), hazardous materials (§3.51), loss of heat sink (§3.54).
- **Ranking criteria basis (§3.19–3.23):** Cross-comparison across all attributes using weighted matrix. May use cost-benefit or differential cost. Can compare against supplier plant parameter envelopes.
- **New installations at existing sites (§3.24–3.27):** Same rigour required. Must assess combined risk, dose constraints, emergency plan interactions, common cause failures.

### Section 4: Classification of Siting Criteria (pp. 20–23, §4.1–4.9)
- **Three classes:** Safety-related, nuclear security, non-safety-related.
- **Safety Set 1 — Natural hazards (§4.3):** Capable faults, ground motion, volcanism, coastal flooding, river flooding, channel blockage, combined flooding, high winds, tornadoes, sand/dust storms, extreme meteorological events, geotechnical hazards, forest fires, combined events.
- **Safety Set 2 — Human induced hazards (§4.4):** Stationary sources (industrial, military), mobile sources (transport, airports, pipelines), electromagnetic interference.
- **Safety Set 3 — Radiological impact (§4.5):** Atmospheric dispersion, surface water dispersion, groundwater dispersion, population density and distribution.
- **Safety Set 4 — Emergency planning (§4.6):** Physical geography, infrastructure, special populations, regulatory zones, concurrent hazard impacts.
- **Non-safety criteria (§4.9):** Cooling water, topography, grid access, environmental impacts, socioeconomic impacts.

### Section 5: Data Necessary at Different Stages (pp. 24–27, §5.1–5.14)
- **Graded data collection (§5.1–5.2):** Regional (coarse) → local (fine) as process progresses.
- **11 database categories (§5.8):** Geological, hydrogeological, seismological, fault displacement, volcanological, geotechnical, coastal flooding, river flooding, meteorological, human induced events, population/land/water/environment.
- **GIS recommended (§5.7).** Site-specific database should be comprehensive and up to date.
- **Climate change (§5.4):** Must consider potential impact on site hazards over plant lifetime.

### Section 6: Siting for Non-NPP Nuclear Installations (pp. 27–29, §6.1–6.9)
- Graded approach based on radiological hazard potential.
- Three or more hazard categories defined per national practice.

### Section 7: Management System (pp. 30–32, §7.1–7.16)
- Work plan, quality management, documentation, traceability, peer review requirements.

### Appendix: Database for the Siting Process (pp. 33–44, §A.1–A.41)
- **Geological/Hydrogeological (§A.3–A.6):** Regional maps, tectonic maps, satellite imagery, borehole logs. Region radius: 150–300 km.
- **Seismological (§A.7–A.9):** Earthquake catalogues, seismic source zones, hazard curves.
- **Fault displacement (§A.10–A.12):** Capable fault investigation, 5 km site vicinity radius.
- **Volcanological (§A.13–A.15):** Holocene volcano history, volcanic products assessment.
- **Geotechnical (§A.16–A.18):** Subsurface conditions, foundation suitability, groundwater.
- **Coastal flooding (§A.19–A.27):** Storm surge, seiche, tsunami, tidal data, shoreline stability.
- **River flooding (§A.28–A.30):** Discharge records, dam break scenarios, ice hazard.
- **Meteorological (§A.31–A.33):** Extreme values, rare events, drainage characteristics.
- **Human induced events (§A.34–A.37):** Industrial, military, transport hazards, screening distances.
- **Population/land/water (§A.38–A.41):** Population density, bio-sensitive areas, emergency plan feasibility.

### Annex I: Tables for the Siting Process (pp. 47–52)
- **Table I-1:** Screening and ranking criteria matrix — categorises each criterion as Exclusionary, Discretionary, or Ranking.
- **Table I-2:** Cross-reference to IAEA safety standards (NS-R-3, NS-G-3.1, NS-G-3.2, SSG-9, SSG-18, SSG-21, NS-G-3.6, NS-G-1.5, NS-G-1.6).

### Annex II: Example Criteria for NPP Siting (pp. 53–60)
- **§II-4:** General site info — maps, EPZ zones (5/16/25/80 km), population distribution.
- **§II-5:** External natural hazards — geology, seismic, volcanic, meteorological, flooding, geotechnical.
- **§II-6:** External human induced hazards — stationary and mobile sources.
- **§II-7:** Radiological impact — dispersion, waste management.
- **§II-8:** Emergency management — evacuation routes, sheltering, transport.
- **§II-9:** Non-safety considerations — topography, accessibility, infrastructure, cooling water, population centres, grid proximity, environmental and socioeconomic impacts.
- **Table II-1: Example screening values:**
  - Distance from capable fault: 8.0 km (Exclusion)
  - Distance from flight paths: 4.0 km (Discretionary)
  - Distance from airport (Type 2): 7.5 km (Discretionary)
  - Distance from small airports: 10.0 km (Discretionary)
  - Distance from large airport: 16.0 km (Discretionary)
  - Distance from military ranges: 30.0 km (Discretionary)
  - Distance from ammunition storage: 8.0 km (Discretionary)
  - Distance from hazmat facilities: 5.0 km (Discretionary)
  - Distance from hazardous cloud sources: 8.0 km (Discretionary)
  - Natural reserves/bio-sensitive: Exclusion zone (Exclusion)
  - Tsunami: 10 km from shore or 50 m above MSL (Discretionary)
